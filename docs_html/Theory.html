3. Theory and Numerical Methods

This chapter summarises the equations FHDeX solves and the main numerical ingredients shared by its solvers. Derivations and analysis are in the method papers listed in References.

3.1. Fluctuating hydrodynamics

Fluctuating hydrodynamics augments each dissipative flux of the continuum equations with a stochastic flux. The noise amplitude is set by the fluctuation–dissipation balance, so that at equilibrium the fields fluctuate with the variances predicted by statistical mechanics. A stochastic flux is written as \(\sqrt{2 L k_B T}\,\mathcal{W}\), where \(L\) is the corresponding transport coefficient (viscosity, diffusivity, conductivity) and \(\mathcal{W}\) is spatio-temporal white noise.

On a grid with cell volume \(\Delta V\) and time step \(\Delta t\), white noise is approximated by independent standard Gaussian random numbers \(W\) scaled by \(1/\sqrt{\Delta V\,\Delta t}\). In 2D, the cell volume includes the out-of-plane thickness cell_depth. The code scales every stochastic flux by variance_coef_mom (momentum) or variance_coef_mass (mass), so setting these to 0 recovers the deterministic equations.

3.2. Incompressible flow

The incompressible fluctuating Navier–Stokes equations for velocity \(\mathbf{v}\) and pressure \(\pi\) are

(3.1)\[\rho\,\partial_t \mathbf{v} + \nabla\pi = -\rho\,\nabla\cdot(\mathbf{v}\mathbf{v}^T) + \nabla\cdot\left(\eta\,\overline{\nabla}\mathbf{v}\right) + \nabla\cdot\boldsymbol{\Sigma} + \mathbf{f}, \qquad \nabla\cdot\mathbf{v} = 0 ,\]

with stochastic stress \(\boldsymbol{\Sigma} = \sqrt{2\eta k_B T}\,\boldsymbol{\mathcal{W}}\). The form of the viscous operator is selected with visc_type. With stoch_stress_form = 1 (symmetric) the noise tensor is \((\boldsymbol{\mathcal{W}}+\boldsymbol{\mathcal{W}}^T)/\sqrt{2}\). The code generates it directly: diagonal entries get variance 2 and off-diagonal entries variance 1 (src_hydro/StochMomFlux.cpp). stoch_stress_form = 0 uses a non-symmetric tensor.

Spatial discretisation

FHDeX uses a staggered (MAC) grid. Scalars and pressure live at cell centres, each velocity component on the faces normal to it, and the off-diagonal stochastic stresses on cell edges (nodes in 2D). Discrete divergence and gradient are adjoint (\(D = -G^T\)), which gives the discrete fluctuation–dissipation balance that makes equilibrium fluctuations correct at all wavenumbers. In the incompressible solvers, advection is either centred or uses the BDS scheme (advection_type).

Temporal integration

exec/hydro uses a predictor–corrector scheme. Viscous terms are treated semi-implicitly (Crank–Nicolson) and advection explicitly, with trapezoidal averaging in the corrector. Each stage requires the solution of a Stokes saddle-point problem (below). algorithm_type selects between the inertial equations and the overdamped (Stokes) limit, where the inertia term is dropped.

3.3. The Stokes solver

Implicit treatment of viscosity together with the incompressibility constraint leads, at every stage, to the linear system

(3.2)\[\begin{split}\begin{pmatrix} A & G \\ -D & 0 \end{pmatrix} \begin{pmatrix} \mathbf{v} \\ \pi \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} \mathbf{b}_u \\ b_p \end{pmatrix}, \qquad A = \theta_\alpha\,\rho/\Delta t - L_\eta ,\end{split}\]

where \(L_\eta\) is the discrete viscous operator and \(\theta_\alpha\) switches the time-derivative (mass) term on or off. src_gmres solves it with restarted, preconditioned GMRES (gmres_max_inner sets the restart length). The preconditioner (precon_type) is a block preconditioner:

  • 1 – projection preconditioner (default); -1 adds a more expensive pressure update;

  • 2/-2 – lower block-triangular;

  • 3/-3 – upper block-triangular;

  • 4/-4 – block diagonal.

The velocity block is approximately inverted by a staggered geometric multigrid V-cycle (StagMGSolver; stag_mg_* parameters, Jacobi or coloured Gauss–Seidel smoothing via stag_mg_smoother). The pressure block uses a cell-centred Poisson solve (MAC projection, mg_* parameters) built on the AMReX MLMG solvers.

3.4. Multispecies mixtures

src_multispec evolves the species densities \(\rho_k\) of an \(N\)-species mixture together with the velocity, in the low-Mach (isothermal, incompressible-like) limit:

(3.3)\[\partial_t \rho_k = -\nabla\cdot(\rho_k\mathbf{v}) - \nabla\cdot\left(\mathbf{F}_k + \widetilde{\mathbf{F}}_k\right), \qquad \sum_k \frac{\rho_k}{\bar\rho_k} = 1 ,\]

where \(\mathbf{F}_k\) are the deterministic diffusive fluxes from the Maxwell–Stefan formulation (coefficients Dbar, thermodynamic factor \(\Gamma\), with \(\Gamma = I\) for is_ideal_mixture = 1) and \(\widetilde{\mathbf{F}}_k\) are the stochastic mass fluxes. The constraint on the pure-component densities rhobar replaces \(\nabla\cdot\mathbf{v} = 0\) and makes the velocity divergence depend on the diffusive fluxes. Correction fluxes keep \(\sum_k \rho_k\) consistent to round-off (correct_flux).

Optional physics:

  • Charged species (use_charged_fluid): electrodiffusive fluxes and a Lorentz force, with the electric potential from a Poisson equation. electroneutral = 1 enforces electroneutrality instead.

  • Multiphase / capillarity (use_multiphase, kc_tension): a reversible stress for surface tension (ComputeDivReversibleStress).

  • Gravity (grav), optionally in the Boussinesq approximation (algorithm_type = 6).

The integrators in exec/multispec are predictor–corrector schemes: algorithm_type = 0 (inertial) and algorithm_type = 6 (Boussinesq). The stochastic mass flux at the midpoint can be interpreted in the Ito or Stratonovich sense (midpoint_stoch_mass_flux_type), and the densities on faces used to weight the noise are averaged as set by avg_type.

3.5. Compressible flow

The compressible solvers evolve the conserved variables

(3.4)\[\mathbf{U} = \left(\rho,\; \mathbf{j} = \rho\mathbf{v},\; \rho E,\; \rho_1,\dots,\rho_N\right)\]

of an ideal-gas mixture,

(3.5)\[\partial_t \mathbf{U} = -\nabla\cdot\left(\mathbf{F}_\text{adv} + \mathbf{F}_\text{diss} + \widetilde{\mathbf{F}}\right) + \mathbf{S},\]

with dissipative fluxes for viscous stress, heat conduction and species diffusion, and the corresponding stochastic fluxes. The primitive variables (velocity, temperature, pressure, mass and mole fractions) are recomputed from \(\mathbf{U}\) after every stage. Transport coefficients for the mixture come from kinetic theory (transport_type, Giovangigli’s formulation by default).

src_compressible_stag stores momentum on cell faces. It advances \(\mathbf{U}\) with the explicit three-stage, third-order Runge–Kutta scheme of Delong et al. (RK3stepStag). Two independent white-noise fields \(W_A, W_B\) are generated per step and combined in each stage as \(W_A + w_2 W_B\), with

(3.6)\[w_2 = \frac{2\sqrt2 + \sqrt3}{5},\qquad \frac{-4\sqrt2 + 3\sqrt3}{5},\qquad \frac{\sqrt2 - 2\sqrt3}{10}\]

in stages 1, 2 and 3. This weighting makes the scheme weakly second-order accurate for the stochastic terms.

3.6. Chemistry and reaction–diffusion

Chemical reactions are specified by stoichiometric coefficients and rate constants (src_chemistry). The number of reaction events in a cell during \(\Delta t\) is sampled according to reaction_type:

  • 0 – deterministic rates;

  • 1 – chemical Langevin equation (Gaussian noise);

  • 2 – SSA (exact stochastic simulation within the cell);

  • 3 – tau leaping (Poisson-distributed event counts).

src_reactDiff couples these reaction samplers to diffusion of number densities. temporal_integrator selects split or unsplit schemes, and reactDiff_diffusion_type the diffusion integrator for split schemes: explicit trapezoidal predictor/corrector (0), Crank–Nicolson (1), explicit midpoint (2), multinomial diffusion (3) or forward Euler (4). Multinomial diffusion moves integer numbers of molecules between neighbouring cells and keeps populations non-negative.

3.7. Particle methods

DSMC

Direct simulation Monte Carlo represents a dilute gas by simulated particles, each standing for particle_neff real molecules. Every step alternates stochastic binary collisions within collision cells with free streaming and surface interactions; reservoir surfaces inject particles (see exec/DSMC – direct simulation Monte Carlo).

Immersed ions

Ions are Brownian particles coupled to the fluctuating Stokes equations. Forces on each ion (electrostatic, short-range, external) are spread to the fluid with a regularised kernel (Peskin kernels of width 3, 4 or 6, or exponential-of-semicircle kernels), and the fluid velocity is interpolated back with the adjoint kernel. Each ion’s diffusion is split into a “wet” part, carried by the resolved fluctuating flow, and a “dry” part, added as independent Brownian motion. Electrostatics use a Poisson solve with a P3M short-range correction, or direct pairwise Coulomb sums (see exec/immersedIons – Brownian ions in a fluctuating fluid).

3.8. Random numbers

All stochastic terms draw from the AMReX random-number generators (Random Numbers) through src_rng. The seed parameter controls reproducibility: a positive value gives a fixed, reproducible sequence (offset by MPI rank), and 0 seeds from the clock.
